# 4 · Market & Competition

*All third-party figures are from public analyst/press sources, listed in §4.7. TAM/SAM/SOM figures for the specific wedge are our estimates with assumptions shown.*

## 4.1 The market you are entering

Three adjacent markets converge in this product:

| Market | Size & growth (public estimates) | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **Data integration** | ~$15–18B in 2025–26, ~13% CAGR to 2030–33 (estimates range $17.2B→$28.6B by 2030; $20.3B→$47.0B by 2033 depending on house) | The plumbing layer — confirms budgets exist and are growing |
| **Data preparation tools** (the exact Alteryx category) | $9.6B (2025) → $11.7B (2026) → $23.7B (2030), ~19% CAGR — with **AI-enabled data preparation explicitly named the growth driver** | Your category. Growing faster than integration overall |
| **BI / analytics platforms** | Large and mature; growth now concentrated in augmented/analytics-AI features | Your output layer — commoditised, which is *good* (you ride it, you don't compete on chart rendering) |

North America is ~35% of integration spend; APAC is the fastest-growing region — relevant if the practice targets GCC/South Asia/SEA groups.

**Read:** the money is flowing toward exactly the capability in your brief (AI doing data prep), but the spend is currently captured by enterprise-priced platforms. The wedge is price-point + delivery model, not new technology categories.

## 4.2 TAM → SAM → SOM for the wedge

**TAM — "AI data prep + governed reporting for organisations without data teams"** (mid-market + groups worldwide). Conservative proxy: ~10% of the data-prep tools market that is mid-market-addressable ≈ **$1.2B+ in 2026, growing ~19%**.

**SAM — groups & mid-market conglomerates in your serviceable regions** (e.g., GCC + South Asia + Africa + SEA; adjust to your footprint). Assumptions: ~25,000 qualifying group structures × realistic $30–80K annual spend on prep+reporting (tools + services blended) ≈ **$0.75–2.0B**.

**SOM — what one consultant-led practice can capture in 3 years:** 15–40 group accounts × $60–150K first-year value (implementation + subscriptions + managed service) ≈ **$1–6M ARR-equivalent**. This is a practice-scale business first, a product-scale business second — deliberately.

## 4.3 Competitive landscape

| Player | What they are | Where they leave your gap |
|---|---|---|
| **Alteryx** | Category-defining data prep; 300+ drag-and-drop tools, 100+ connectors, 8,300+ customers; $970M revenue (2023); taken private by Clearlake + Insight for $4.4B (closed Mar 2024) | Analyst-first: someone must *build* workflows. Enterprise pricing; buyer profile is CDO/VP-Analytics at 500+ headcount orgs with $100–500K analytics budgets. PE ownership historically precedes pricing discipline |
| **Microsoft Fabric + Power BI Copilot** | Unifying the Microsoft estate; Copilot now on all paid SKUs; "data agents" answer NL questions over OneLake respecting permissions | Assumes the Microsoft estate *and* modelled data. The modelling/cleaning gap is exactly what mid-market groups can't staff. Strong where IT is already Microsoft-mature |
| **Databricks** | Engineering-first lakehouse; Agent Bricks, MLflow, vector search, AI/BI Genie | Needs a platform team; targets AI-native enterprises. Not a group-CFO purchase |
| **Snowflake (Cortex, Snowflake Intelligence)** | Governed AI data cloud; SQL-first AI functions; NL assistant GA | Same shape: warehouse-first, team-required |
| **Talend / Informatica / Qlik** | Enterprise integration & quality suites | Heavy, expensive, sold to IT — not to group finance |
| **Fivetran / Airbyte / dbt** | Pipelines & transformation | Assume a target model and an owner; pipes, not outcomes |
| **ThoughtSpot / NL-BI layers** | Search-driven analytics | Sit *on top of* a warehouse; don't build one |
| **Boutique Excel-automation consultancies** | Power Query / VBA / Python macros | Bespoke, unmaintainable, no product, no governance — your competitor in every deal, and beatable on repeatability |

**Structural insight:** every serious incumbent sells to organisations that *already have data capability*. The segment that has only Excel and a CFO is structurally underserved by all of them simultaneously. That is your market.

## 4.4 Positioning

**For group leadership:** *"Send us your spreadsheets. We'll send you the truth."* — One governed surface for every entity, department and currency; every number traceable to its source file.

**For the entity FD:** *"Stop reformatting. Just drop the file."*

**For IT/security:** *"Enterprise governance without an enterprise data team."* — RLS by entity, full audit trail, regional hosting.

**Differentiators, ranked by defensibility:**
1. **Consultant-led outcome delivery** — incumbents ship software; you ship a working group reporting capability. The competition for the CFO's budget is often an internal Excel habit, not another vendor.
2. **Compounding industry mapping knowledge base** — every confirmed mapping makes the next onboarding cheaper; switching away means re-teaching a competitor's tool everything you already taught ours.
3. **Zero-template ingestion with a human gate** — the *trustworthy* version of "any file," vs. pure-auto tools that fail silently.
4. **Group-native data model** — entities, departments, consolidation and FX in the core, not bolted on.
5. **Regional presence & language** — a real factor in GCC/emerging-market groups.

## 4.5 Pricing strategy (illustrative — validate in first 3 deals)

| Component | Model | Illustrative range | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Platform subscription | Per group + per entity | $12–30K/yr group base + $4–10K/yr per entity | Undercuts the $100K+ enterprise analytics budget bar while signalling substance |
| Implementation | Fixed-fee packages | $15–60K per phase (see §5 packages) | The consulting revenue line |
| Managed service | Monthly retainer | $3–10K/mo | "Data office as a service" — the annuity |
| Mapping/onboarding sessions | Per entity | $2–6K one-off | Tier-3 files become billable scope, honestly |

Anchor against the customer's current state: weeks of analyst time per month across 10+ entities typically dwarfs the subscription. Sell the *consolidation days saved*, not the software.

## 4.6 Market risks from the competitive picture

- **Microsoft could commoditise the entry level.** Fabric Copilot on all paid SKUs plus data agents is aimed at self-service. Mitigation: be the delivery layer that *uses* such platforms where present, and own the group semantic model + service relationship regardless of stack.
- **"AI will just do it all" expectations.** Agentic-AI reality today: agents still struggle with ambiguity and need human-control frameworks. Position the human gate as maturity, not limitation.
- **Category confusion.** "Is it ETL? BI? A data warehouse?" — answer with the demo, not taxonomy.
